Cluster centres (k-medoids on the 50-D embedding)

  • 2. Ingleton's inequality (81 nodes)
  • 3. Monstrous moonshine (31 nodes)
  • 4. Nielsen–Ninomiya theorem (32 nodes)

Circled numbers on the graph mark the medoid of each cluster. Colour is a soft blend of the three nearest centre colours, weighted by exp(−d²/σ), so a node between two mathematical areas gets a between colour instead of being forced into one.

How the colour was built

  1. Embed: TF-IDF over title + lead summary (sublinear tf, idf, L2 normalised), then a truncated SVD to 50 dimensions — LSA. Offline, no model download.
  2. Centres: k-medoids (PAM-lite, 25 iterations) on those 50 dimensions, so a centre is an actual article rather than an average.
  3. Colour: for each node, distances to all centres → exp(−d²/σ) weights → keep the 3 strongest → blend their palette colours.
  4. Compare: the button above also offers the plain PCA→RGB projection from the brief, so the two strategies can be judged side by side.

Honest caveats. The embedding is bag-of-words over short texts: it captures vocabulary, not mathematical structure, so two unrelated notions that share boilerplate ("theorem", "proof", "group") will sit close. The clusters are therefore lexical families, not a classification of mathematics. And the positions are inherited from ForceAtlas2 on the full graph — the measurement chose the node subset, not a new layout.
